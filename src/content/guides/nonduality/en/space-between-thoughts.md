# The Space Between Thoughts

> Noticing the gap and recognizing spacious awareness

*Attention naturally fixates on thoughts—the words, images, and mental activity that fill awareness. But what about the space in which thoughts arise? What about the gap between one thought and the next? This article invites you to shift attention from the content of thinking to the context that contains it.*

## The Usual Focus

Most of the time, attention is absorbed in thoughts:

- Planning the day
- Remembering the past
- Solving problems
- Imagining scenarios
- Commenting on experience
- Judging and evaluating

**Thoughts aren't a problem.** They're useful, creative, necessary. The issue is exclusive identification with them—thinking you ARE your thoughts, that the stream of thinking is what you fundamentally are.

**This inquiry offers a different possibility:** What if you're not the thoughts but the space in which they arise?

## The First Gap

Let's start with a simple observation.

**Right now, as you read:**
- Thoughts are arising
- Perhaps thoughts about these words
- Perhaps unrelated thoughts drifting through
- Perhaps meta-thoughts about having thoughts

**Now notice:**

Between thoughts, there's a gap. However brief. A moment where one thought has ended and the next hasn't yet begun.

**Try this:**
1. Close your eyes
2. Watch for the next thought to arise
3. Notice the moment just before it appears
4. That moment—what's there?

**Take a moment to actually try this before reading on.**

## What's In The Gap?

When you look for the space between thoughts, you might notice:

**Silence**
Not the absence of sound, but the absence of mental chatter. A quiet that's full rather than empty.

**Spaciousness**
A sense of openness, room, breathing space. Like a sky with no clouds.

**Presence**
Something alert, awake, here—but without content. Awareness itself, prior to what it's aware of.

**Peace**
Not the peace that comes from resolved problems, but a fundamental okayness that's already present.

**Or you might notice:**
"I can't find a gap—my thoughts are continuous!"

**That's okay.** The gaps are often very brief. And sometimes, the looking itself generates thoughts about looking. Keep practicing—the gaps will become more apparent.

## The Paradox of Looking

Here's something interesting that happens:

When you try to catch the gap, thinking often speeds up. You think: "Is this the gap?" "Am I noticing it?" "Did I miss it?"

**These are thoughts ABOUT the gap, not the gap itself.**

The gap isn't an object you grasp. It's more like... it grasps you. Or rather, you realize you've always been it.

**The shift is subtle:**
- From trying to catch the gap
- To recognizing you're already the space in which gaps appear (and thoughts too)

## A Different Approach: Lengthening the Gap

Instead of trying to catch fleeting gaps, you can create conditions where the space becomes more apparent.

### Practice 1: The Breath as Bridge

**Instructions:**
1. Sit comfortably, eyes closed or soft-focused
2. Notice the natural rhythm of breathing
3. At the end of each exhale, there's a natural pause
4. Don't force it—just notice it
5. In that pause, before the next inhale, what's here?
6. Rest in that pause without trying to extend it

**What you might notice:**
- The pause contains awareness but no thought
- There's alert presence in the stillness
- The gap is actually full, not empty

### Practice 2: The Space After a Question

**Instructions:**
1. Ask yourself a question without trying to answer it
2. "What am I?"
3. Then wait...
4. Don't reach for an answer
5. Stay with the question hanging in space
6. What's in that space of not-knowing?

**What you might notice:**
- Presence without answers
- Awareness that doesn't need content
- The difference between thinking and being aware

### Practice 3: Noticing Natural Gaps

**Throughout your day:**
- When you finish a task, notice the moment before starting the next
- When someone finishes speaking, notice the gap before you respond
- When you complete an exhale, notice before you inhale
- When you close a book, notice before you stand up

**These natural transitions are gaps.** They're always there. You just haven't been paying attention to them.

## What The Space Reveals

As you become familiar with the gaps, you might start to notice:

### 1. Thoughts Come From The Space

Thoughts don't create themselves. They arise from somewhere.

**Where do they come from?**

If you watch carefully, you'll see thoughts emerging from the space of awareness—like bubbles rising from the bottom of a pond.

**And where do they go?**

They dissolve back into the same space.

**What remains?** The space itself—constant, unchanging, always here.

### 2. You Are Not Located In Thoughts

When you're identified with thoughts, it feels like "you" are in your head, thinking.

But when you recognize the space between thoughts, something shifts. **"You" are revealed as the space itself—spacious awareness that contains thinking rather than being contained by it.**

This is profoundly liberating. Thoughts can come and go, but the space that you are remains undisturbed.

### 3. The Space Is Not Empty

At first, the gap might seem like nothing—just absence of thought.

But look more carefully.

The space isn't empty. It's:
- **Aware** (otherwise you couldn't notice the gap)
- **Luminous** (there's a quality of knowing or presence)
- **Spacious** (infinite room for whatever arises)
- **Peaceful** (not because nothing's happening, but because there's no resistance)

This is awareness itself—what you fundamentally are.

### 4. The Gap Is Always Here

The gap isn't somewhere else or sometime else. **It's here, now, in this very moment.**

Even while reading these words, even while thinking about them, the spacious awareness that contains the reading and thinking is present.

**You don't need to wait for a gap to recognize the space.** The space is here whether thoughts are present or absent.

## Common Experiences

### "My Mind Is Too Busy"

If your mind feels very active, that's not a problem. It's actually perfect for this practice because the contrast between thoughts and space becomes more apparent.

**Don't try to stop thinking.** Just notice the gaps, however brief. Even a fraction of a second reveals the space.

### "I Can't Find Any Gaps"

Try the breath practice. The natural pause after exhale is always there—you just might not have noticed it before.

Or try this: Count your thoughts. "One... two... three..." To count them, you must recognize when one ends and another begins. That recognition happens in the gap.

### "The Gap Disappears When I Look For It"

Yes! Because looking FOR it is another thought.

**The shift:** From looking FOR the gap to recognizing you're already the space in which gaps (and thoughts) appear.

You can't find the space because you ARE the space.

### "It Feels Like Nothing Special"

Good! That ordinariness is actually the recognition.

The space isn't a special state to achieve. It's the ordinary, always-present awareness that you are. Nothing exotic. Nothing dramatic. Just... this.

**Don't dismiss the ordinary.** It's what you've been looking for all along.

### "The Space Seems Vast and Unlimited"

Some people experience the space as boundless—like recognizing you're not contained in the body but are the space in which the body appears.

If this happens, don't grasp it or try to make it return. Just notice it when it's here.

**But even if you don't experience vastness, the space is still here.** The recognition doesn't depend on particular experiences.

## The Space Holds Everything

Here's something profound:

The space between thoughts isn't separate from thoughts. It's not that thoughts are bad and space is good. It's not that you should try to stay in the space and avoid thoughts.

**The space contains and allows everything:**
- Thoughts and silence
- Activity and rest
- Clarity and confusion
- Peace and agitation

All of it arises in the same spacious awareness. All of it is allowed. All of it is held.

**You are not the thoughts OR the space.**
**You are the awareness that contains both.**

## Integrating This Recognition

As this recognition stabilizes, you might notice:

**In meditation:**
- Less struggle with busy mind
- Recognition that the space is always here
- Thoughts become less sticky, less compelling
- Natural settling without forcing

**In daily life:**
- Micro-gaps between activities
- Brief moments of spaciousness in the midst of doing
- Less compulsive thinking
- More presence, even when thinking

**In difficult moments:**
- Even strong emotions arise in space
- There's room for whatever's happening
- The space itself is never disturbed
- This provides fundamental stability

## A Daily Practice

**Morning:**
Start your day by noticing the space. Before getting out of bed, before checking your phone, spend 30 seconds just resting in the gap between sleep and activity.

**Throughout the day:**
Notice transition moments:
- Between tasks
- Between conversations
- Between breaths
- Between thoughts

Just brief moments of recognition: "Ah, space."

**Evening:**
End your day by recognizing the space. As you prepare for sleep, notice the gap between waking and sleeping. Rest there.

## The Practice Deepens

Initially, you might only notice the space occasionally—brief gaps when thinking slows down.

Over time, you might recognize:
- The space is here even when thinking is active
- You can be aware of thoughts AND the space simultaneously
- The space isn't elsewhere or elsewhen—it's this, right now
- What you are isn't contained in time or space—it IS the space

**But don't wait for this deepening.** Each moment of recognition—however brief—is already the recognition.

## When Thoughts Return

After resting in the space, thoughts inevitably return. This isn't a problem. This isn't failure.

**Thoughts returning is like:**
- Clouds appearing in the sky (the sky remains)
- Waves arising in the ocean (the ocean remains)
- Words appearing on a blank page (the page remains)

**The space doesn't leave when thoughts arrive.** It never left. You just stopped noticing it.

**The practice:** Notice the space again. And again. And again.

## The Recognition

At some point—maybe suddenly, maybe gradually—you might recognize:

**You're not trying to GET to the space.**
**You're not trying to CREATE gaps.**
**You're not trying to achieve some special state.**

**You ARE the space.**

Always have been.
Always will be.

The thoughts come and go in you.
The gaps come and go in you.
Everything arises and passes in the spacious awareness that you are.

**This moment, right now:**

There's awareness.
It's spacious.
It's here.
It's what you are.

Can you notice it?
