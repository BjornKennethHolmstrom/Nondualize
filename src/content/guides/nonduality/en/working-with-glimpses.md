# Working with Glimpses

> Integration practices after moments of recognition

*You've been working with the inquiries and practices in Section 2. Perhaps you've had moments—brief or extended—where something shifted. A glimpse of what these teachings point to. A moment of recognizing what you are. Then... it seemed to fade. You're "back" to the usual sense of separate self. This is completely normal. This article is about what to do with glimpses, how to work with the oscillation between recognition and forgetting, and how insights gradually stabilize into lived reality.*

## What Is a Glimpse?

**A glimpse is a moment when:**
- The usual sense of separate self loosens or dissolves
- What you fundamentally are becomes briefly obvious
- The boundary between self and other feels transparent
- There's recognition of awareness as what you are
- The present moment is seen as sufficient, complete

**Glimpses can feel:**
- Profound and life-changing
- Surprisingly ordinary ("oh, is that all?")
- Peaceful and expansive
- Disorienting or groundless
- Crystal clear or subtly unclear

**Duration varies:**
- A few seconds
- Several minutes
- Hours (rare for initial glimpses)
- In and out throughout a day

**They often happen:**
- During meditation or inquiry
- In nature
- In moments of relaxation
- Upon waking
- In crisis (when the usual structures collapse)
- Spontaneously, for no apparent reason

## The Most Common Mistake

**The mistake:** Trying to get it back.

**What happens:**
1. You have a glimpse—clear, obvious, liberating
2. It fades or becomes less clear
3. You think: "I had it and lost it"
4. You try to recreate the experience
5. Now there's a "you" seeking something you don't have
6. This reinforces the very separation you glimpsed through

**The pattern:**
- Glimpse → Recognition → "I got it!" → Identity around "getting it" → Separate self reconstituted → Seeking to get it back

**This is so common it's almost universal.**

## What Actually Happened

**More accurate understanding:**

**You didn't "have" something that you then "lost."**

What happened:
1. Recognition occurred
2. The mind created a story: "I recognized"
3. This creates a "me" who recognized (and might not recognize again)
4. The seeking resumes

**The truth:**
- Awareness never left
- Recognition never stopped
- What came and went was the clarity of recognition
- What you are didn't change—only the clarity of seeing it

**You can't lose what you are.**

You can only overlook it, forget it, get absorbed in thoughts and miss it.

**The glimpse isn't something you achieved.** It's a momentary seeing of what's always been true.

## The Oscillation

**What's normal:**
- Recognition → Forgetting → Recognition → Forgetting

**This oscillation is not a problem.** It's part of the process of stabilization.

**Think of it like:**
- Learning to ride a bike: balance → wobble → balance → wobble
- Eventually the wobbles decrease
- Balance becomes more natural, less effortful
- But there's no moment when you "finally got it forever"—it's ongoing

**With non-dual recognition:**
- Clear seeing → absorbed in thought → clear seeing → absorbed in thought
- Gradually: the seeing becomes more continuous
- The forgetting becomes briefer
- But complete, permanent stability is rare

**Don't expect:** One final recognition after which you never forget again.

**More realistic:** Increasing familiarity, more frequent recognition, quicker return when you notice you've forgotten.

## Working With Glimpses Skillfully

### 1. Appreciate Without Grasping

**When a glimpse happens:**

**Do:** Notice it, appreciate it, allow it

**Don't:** Try to hold onto it, make it into something special, create an identity around it

**The practice:**
- "Ah, there it is" (recognition)
- Let it be as it is
- Don't chase when it fades
- Trust it will return

**Why this matters:** Grasping creates tension. Tension obscures recognition. Non-grasping allows natural stabilization.

### 2. Notice What Remains Constant

**During a glimpse:**

What's present? Awareness? Spaciousness? Presence? Peace?

**After the glimpse fades:**

Look again. Is that actually gone? Or is it still here, just not noticed?

**Often what happens:**
- The intensity of recognition fades
- But awareness itself never left
- It was just overlooked again

**The practice:**
- Don't compare now to the glimpse
- Look freshly: What's here right now?
- Often you'll find: awareness is still present, still aware

**This reveals:** The glimpse didn't give you something new. It revealed what's always here.

### 3. Don't Create Hierarchy

**The trap:**
- "That glimpse was real recognition"
- "This normal state is illusion"
- "I need to get back to the real thing"

**More accurate:**
- Recognition and non-recognition are both happening in awareness
- Clear seeing and forgetting are both arising in what you are
- There's no hierarchy—just more or less clarity

**The practice:**
- Don't reject this moment because it's not "glimpse clarity"
- This moment too is arising in awareness
- Can you recognize awareness even when the clarity isn't as sharp?

### 4. Integrate, Don't Isolate

**The mistake:** Thinking glimpses only happen in special conditions:
- During meditation
- In nature
- When alone
- When quiet

**The integration:**
- Can recognition happen during conversation?
- While working?
- In difficult emotions?
- In mundane activities?

**The practice:**
- Intentionally bring the inquiry into daily life
- Notice what's aware while doing dishes
- Recognize awareness during conversation
- Find the space even in chaos

**Why:** If recognition only happens in "spiritual" contexts, it's not truly integrated. It needs to be available in all of life.

### 5. Work With the Return of Self

**After a glimpse, the separate self often returns—sometimes with a vengeance:**
- Doubt: "Did that really happen? Maybe I imagined it"
- Seeking: "I need to get back there"
- Claiming: "I'm awakened now" (spiritual ego)
- Disappointment: "Why did it fade?"

**All of these are thoughts arising in awareness.**

**The practice:**
- Notice these thoughts
- Don't fight them or believe them
- Notice what's aware of them
- Rest there

**The self-sense returning isn't a problem.** It's just another wave in awareness. Don't make it solid by resisting it or believing it.

## Common Post-Glimpse Experiences

### The Hangover

**Sometimes after a deep glimpse:**
- Fatigue
- Disorientation
- Difficulty focusing
- Emotional sensitivity
- Need for rest

**This is normal.** The usual structures of self have been loosened. Give yourself time to reorient.

**What helps:**
- Rest, sleep
- Gentle activity
- Time in nature
- Simple routines
- Not trying to "figure out what happened"

**Let integration happen naturally.**

### The Dry Period

**Sometimes after glimpses:**
- A period where recognition seems completely unavailable
- The practices feel dead
- Nothing is working
- "Did I lose it?"

**This is also normal.** It's often the integration phase.

**What's happening:**
- The insight is digesting
- Deeper structures are reorganizing
- The recognition is stabilizing at a subtler level

**What helps:**
- Keep practicing gently
- Don't force
- Trust the process
- Sometimes doing nothing is the practice

**This dry period often precedes deeper stabilization.**

### The Inflation

**Sometimes after glimpses:**
- A sense of being special: "I got it!"
- Wanting to teach others (before it's stabilized)
- Subtle superiority: "I see what others don't"
- Talking about the experience frequently

**This is spiritual ego—the separate self claiming the recognition.**

**What helps:**
- Notice it without judgment (it's very common)
- Keep practicing
- Stay humble (there's always more to integrate)
- If you're talking about it constantly, practice silence
- Remember: if "you" got enlightened, there's still a "you"

**The inflation usually deflates naturally. Just don't act on it in ways you'll regret.**

### The Integration Phase

**The healthiest post-glimpse experience:**
- Quiet integration
- Not making a big deal of it
- Continuing practice
- Noticing subtle changes in daily life
- Not needing to talk about it
- Being okay with not knowing

**Signs of healthy integration:**
- More present, less in head
- Easier with emotions
- Less reactive
- More humor
- Less need to prove anything
- Gentler with self and others

## Deepening Recognition Over Time

### Frequent Brief Returns

**More effective than:**
- Rare long recognitions

**Practice:**
- Many times daily
- Brief moments (even seconds)
- Return to awareness frequently
- Build familiarity

**Why:** Like strengthening a muscle. Frequent use creates stability.

### Shorter Recovery Time

**At first:**
- Forget for hours, days, weeks
- Takes effort to return

**With practice:**
- Notice forgetting more quickly
- Return more easily
- The gap between forgetting and remembering shrinks

**This is progress—not complete stability, but increasing fluidity.**

### Recognition in Difficulty

**Big milestone:**
- Not just in peace, but in challenge
- Not just in meditation, but in conflict
- Not just alone, but in relationship
- Not just in clarity, but in confusion

**When you can recognize awareness even when:**
- Strongly triggered
- In pain
- In confusion
- In fear

**Then the recognition is stabilizing.**

Not because these don't arise, but because they're held in awareness without consuming you completely.

### The Ordinariness

**A sign of maturity:**

Recognition becomes less "special."

**At first:** Glimpses feel profound, significant, life-changing

**Later:** Recognition is just... obvious, ordinary, not a big deal

**This ordinariness is actually deeper stabilization:**
- Not seeking special states
- Aware that awareness is always here
- No longer making enlightenment into a thing

**The extraordinary becomes ordinary. The ordinary becomes extraordinary.**

## Practices for Stabilization

### The Morning Set

**Each morning:**
1. Before getting up, notice awareness
2. Notice it's here before any thought of "me"
3. Brief recognition: aware, aware, aware
4. Carry this into the first activities

**Why:** Starting the day from recognition sets a different tone.

### The Anchor Activities

**Choose 3-5 daily activities as anchors:**
- Making coffee
- Brushing teeth
- Opening doors
- Checking phone
- Washing hands

**Each time you do these:**
- Pause briefly
- Notice what's aware
- Return to recognition

**This builds:** Dozens of return points throughout the day.

### The Hourly Bell

**Set a gentle reminder every hour:**
- Pause
- Notice what's aware right now
- Three breaths resting as awareness
- Continue

**Over weeks/months:** This creates a rhythm of remembering.

### Working With Forgetting

**When you notice you've forgotten:**

**Don't:** Beat yourself up, feel like you failed

**Do:** 
- Celebrate noticing! (That's already awareness)
- Return gently
- Notice what's aware right now
- Continue

**The noticing that you forgot IS the recognition returning.**

### The Evening Review

**Before sleep:**
- Review the day
- Notice: When did recognition happen?
- When did you forget?
- What helped recognition emerge?
- What obscured it?

**No judgment—just noticing patterns.**

**This builds:** Increasing familiarity with the rhythms of recognition and forgetting.

## The Long View

### Months 1-3: The Honeymoon

**Typical pattern:**
- Excitement about glimpses
- Lots of practice
- Ups and downs
- Enthusiasm and frustration

**What helps:**
- Stay steady with practice
- Don't get discouraged by forgetting
- Don't get inflated by glimpses
- Keep it simple

### Months 3-12: The Integration

**Typical pattern:**
- Less dramatic experiences
- More subtle recognition
- Periods of dryness
- Gradual stabilization

**What helps:**
- Trust the process
- Keep practicing even when it feels "dead"
- Notice small changes in daily life
- Don't measure progress by intensity of experience

### Years 1-3: The Maturation

**Typical pattern:**
- Recognition more available
- Less seeking
- More ordinariness
- Continuing refinement

**What helps:**
- Don't think you're "done"
- Keep practicing
- Notice remaining blind spots
- Stay humble

### Ongoing: The Living

**Eventually:**
- Less sense of "practicing" something
- More just... living from recognition
- Still periods of forgetting (usually briefer)
- Less concern about whether you're "there" or not

**This isn't the end—it's the ongoing unfolding.**

## Common Questions

### "How do I know if I've had a real glimpse?"

**You'll know.** There's a quality of recognition that's unmistakable in the moment.

**But:** Don't get obsessed with determining if it was "real." What matters is whether it changes how you relate to life.

**Better question:** "Is my practice helping me suffer less and be more present?"

### "Can I lose the recognition permanently?"

**No.** What you are doesn't come and go.

**But:** Clarity of recognition can seem to disappear for periods. This is normal. It returns.

**Even if:** Years pass and you feel you've completely lost it—the recognition is available right now. Just look: What's aware?

### "Should I seek more glimpses?"

**Not exactly.**

**Better:**
- Practice regularly
- Create conditions (meditation, inquiry, stillness)
- But don't grasp for results
- Let recognition emerge naturally

**Seeking glimpses reinforces seeking.** Just practice. Glimpses happen when they happen.

### "What if glimpses stop happening?"

**This can mean:**
- The recognition is stabilizing more subtly
- You're in an integration phase
- The practice needs refreshing
- You're trying too hard

**What to do:**
- Keep practicing gently
- Try a different approach from Section 2
- Take a break and just live
- Trust the process

### "Can I talk about my glimpses?"

**Generally:**
- Talk less than you think you should
- Choose listeners carefully (people who understand)
- Notice if talking reinforces ego
- Silence often supports integration better

**But:** Having one person (teacher, friend, therapist) to process with can be helpful.

### "Is this the same as enlightenment?"

**Glimpses are not enlightenment** (if by enlightenment we mean permanent, complete stabilization).

**But:** They're recognition of what teachings point to. They're the beginning of the path of living from recognition.

**Don't worry about terminology.** Just practice, integrate, live.

## The Paradox of Stabilization

**Here's something interesting:**

The more you try to stabilize recognition, the more elusive it becomes (because trying creates a seeker).

**But:** The more you practice and integrate, the more it naturally stabilizes.

**The balance:**
- Practice diligently
- But hold it lightly
- Do the work
- But don't grasp the results

**Trust that:**
- Awareness is always here
- Recognition will strengthen naturally
- You don't need to force it
- The unfolding happens at its own pace

## Right Now

**Stop trying to "work with glimpses."**

**Right now:**

Is awareness present? Yes.

That's it. That's the recognition.

**Not:** "I need to have a glimpse"

**But:** Awareness is already here, already recognized.

**Every moment of noticing awareness is already the recognition you're seeking to stabilize.**

**It's this simple. And this ongoing.**
