# Questions That Dissolve the Questioner

> Working with koans and paradoxical inquiries

*Some questions aren't meant to be answered. They're meant to undo the questioner. These are not riddles with clever solutions—they're invitations to a different kind of seeing. When the conceptual mind exhausts itself trying to answer, something else can emerge. This article offers traditional and contemporary paradoxical questions that point beyond themselves.*

## The Nature of These Questions

**These are not:**
- Logical puzzles to solve
- Riddles with hidden answers
- Tests of spiritual knowledge
- Intellectual exercises

**These are:**
- Invitations to look beyond concepts
- Tools to exhaust the conceptual mind
- Gateways to direct recognition
- Questions that consume themselves

**How to work with them:**
1. Hold the question without rushing to answer
2. Let it work on you rather than you working on it
3. Notice when the mind tries to "figure it out"
4. Stay with the not-knowing
5. Let the question dissolve into direct seeing

## Traditional Zen Koans

### "What was your face before your parents were born?"

**Don't think about this. Actually look.**

Right now, what are you before all personal history?

Before your name, your story, your memories—what's here?

**The mind will try to answer:**
- "Pure consciousness"
- "Nothing"
- "Everything"

**But those are concepts.** What's being pointed to is prior to all concepts, including the concept of "prior."

**Sit with this question.** Let it work on you.

Not "What do I think the answer is?" but "What am I before thought?"

---

**What some people discover:**
- A sense of timeless presence
- Awareness without personal history
- Just this, prior to all story
- The question dissolves because there's no separate questioner

**If nothing happens, that's fine.** Keep living with the question. It might ripen suddenly or gradually.

### "What is the sound of one hand clapping?"

**The mind immediately wants to solve this:**
- "Silence"
- "It's impossible—clapping requires two hands"
- "It's a trick question"

**But none of these are the answer.** Because there is no conceptual answer.

**Instead:**

Hold up one hand. Move it as if clapping. 

What's actually happening?

Not your thought about what's happening. The direct experience.

**The point isn't to find "the answer."** The point is to exhaust the mind's need to answer, until you're simply present to what is.

**Some teachers have responded to this koan by:**
- Clapping once
- Holding up one hand silently
- Making a sound
- Saying nothing

**None of these are "the" answer.** Each demonstrates a way of meeting the question that doesn't get trapped in conceptual thinking.

### "Does a dog have Buddha nature?"

A monk asked master Zhaozhou: "Does a dog have Buddha nature?"

Zhaozhou answered: "Mu" (無 - meaning "no," "nothing," "not," or "without").

**But this seems to contradict basic Buddhist teaching:** All beings have Buddha nature.

**The koan isn't asking for theological consistency.** It's pointing to something else entirely.

**Work with "Mu":**

Sit with this non-answer. Let it fill your awareness.

Not thinking about what "Mu" means. Just: Mu. Mu. Mu.

**What some people discover:**
- The question itself dissolves
- The separation between questioner and question collapses
- What remains is just this—no need for answers
- "Mu" becomes a gateway to no-thing-ness

### "Show me your original face"

This is a command, not a question. But it functions similarly.

**Don't think about what your original face is.**

**Right now, show it.**

Not the face in the mirror. Not your personality. Not your self-concept.

Your original face—what you are before all concepts.

**Can you show it?**

**The traditional story:** A student struggled with this koan for months. Finally, exhausted, he let go completely. In that moment of surrender, the teacher said, "There—that's it."

What was it? Not something the student did or said. The moment of letting go itself revealed what had always been there.

## Contemporary Paradoxical Questions

### "Who is aware of awareness?"

**Set it up:**
- You're aware right now
- Notice that you're aware
- Now: Who or what is aware of that awareness?

**The mind tries to answer:**
- "I am"
- "Consciousness is"
- "Awareness is aware of itself"

**But look more carefully:**

If you're aware of awareness, there must be an observer of awareness. But then who observes the observer? And who observes that?

**The infinite regress reveals:**

There is no separate observer of awareness. Awareness is aware of itself. Or more accurately: there's just awareness, appearing as if there's an observer and an observed.

**Sit with this:**

Can you find the one who's aware of awareness?

Or is there just awareness, aware, with no separate knower?

### "Can you step outside the present moment?"

**Try it:**

Try to leave the present moment.

Think about the past—but that thinking happens now.
Imagine the future—but that imagining happens now.
Try to escape "now"—but that trying happens now.

**The discovery:** You can never leave the present moment. There's nowhere else to go.

**But then:**

If you can never leave now, and you've never been anywhere else, what is this "now" that's so constant, so inescapable?

**And who is it that's always in the present moment?**

Or is there no separate "who"—just the present moment being itself?

### "What are you when you're not thinking about what you are?"

**Usually:**
- You have thoughts about yourself
- Those thoughts create a sense of identity
- "I am this kind of person"
- "I am someone who..."

**But right now:**

Stop thinking about what you are.

In the gap before the next thought about yourself—what are you?

**Don't answer conceptually.**

Just notice: when there's no thought of "me," what's here?

**Is anything missing?**

Or is there a sense of being, presence, awareness—completely independent of any thought about what you are?

### "Where do thoughts come from, and where do they go?"

**Watch thoughts arising:**

Before a thought appears, where is it?

When it disappears, where does it go?

**The mind wants to answer:**
- "From the unconscious"
- "Into memory"
- "From brain activity"

**But experientially:**

Thoughts appear from... nowhere you can locate. They disappear into... nowhere you can find.

**They arise in awareness. They dissolve in awareness.**

**But then:** Are thoughts separate from awareness? Or are they awareness appearing as thought?

Is there awareness here, and thoughts there? Or just awareness appearing as thinking?

### "What is this?"

**This is perhaps the most direct question.**

Not "What is the meaning of this?"
Not "What should I call this?"

Simply: What is this?

**This moment. This experience. This.**

**Don't name it. Don't interpret it. Just:**

What is this?

**The mind will try to answer:**
- "It's my experience"
- "It's reality"
- "It's consciousness"

**But those are concepts about this. What is this itself, before you name it?**

**Live with this question.** Let it become your primary inquiry.

In every moment: What is this?

Not seeking an answer. Just staying with the question until the question and the questioner dissolve into... this.

## Self-Referential Paradoxes

### "Can you see your own eyes?"

**Without a mirror:**

Try to see your eyes.

You can see everything else, but not your eyes themselves.

**Similarly:**

Can awareness be aware of itself as an object?

Or is awareness always the subject, never the object?

**And yet:** You're aware that you're aware. So in some sense, awareness is aware of itself.

**The paradox points to:** Awareness isn't a thing that can be observed. It's what's doing the observing. The seer can't be seen—yet somehow, awareness knows itself.

### "What is it that never changes?"

**Everything changes:**
- Body changes
- Thoughts change
- Emotions change
- Perceptions change
- Even the sense of self changes

**But:** What's aware of all this change?

**Does awareness itself change?**

Or is there something about awareness that remains constant while everything else transforms?

**Look for yourself:**

The content of awareness is always changing. But the awareness itself—can you find a moment when it wasn't present?

**And if it's always present, never changing—what is it?**

### "If everything is awareness, what's aware of awareness?"

**The setup:**

If non-duality is true, everything is awareness appearing in different forms.

**But then:** What's aware of awareness?

**The paradox:**
- If something is aware of awareness, they're two separate things (not non-dual)
- If awareness is aware of itself, how is that possible?
- Can something be both subject and object simultaneously?

**This question short-circuits the subject-object framework.**

There is no answer within dualistic thinking. The question only resolves when the dualistic framework itself dissolves.

**What remains:** Just awareness, knowing itself, with no separation between knower and known.

## Questions About Existence

### "Why is there something rather than nothing?"

**This ancient philosophical question becomes a non-dual inquiry:**

Right now, notice that existence IS.

Something is happening. There is experience.

**But why?**

Not in the causal sense (what created it), but in the more fundamental sense: Why existence at all?

**The question has no answer—and that's the point.**

Being simply IS. Existence doesn't need justification.

**And you are that existence, looking at itself, asking why it is.**

The questioner and the questioned are not separate.

### "What exists between your thoughts?"

**We explored gaps between thoughts earlier. But now:**

In that gap, does anything exist?

**The usual answer:** "Yes, awareness exists."

**But go deeper:** Is "awareness" a thing that exists? Or is it the context in which existence/non-existence appear?

**The paradox:**
- If nothing exists between thoughts, how do you know?
- If something exists, what is it?
- Is existence itself more fundamental than any particular thing?

### "Are you here now, or is 'here' and 'now' what you are?"

**Usually we think:** I am a person who is here, who is experiencing now.

**But investigate:**

Can you be anywhere other than here?
Can you be at any time other than now?

**If not:**

Maybe you're not IN the here-now. Maybe here-now is what you are.

**Not a being occupying space and time, but space and time themselves, knowing themselves as here, as now.**

## Working With Impossible Questions

### How to Practice

**1. Choose one question**

Don't try to work with all of them. Pick one that resonates, that needles you, that won't let you go.

**2. Live with it**

Not for five minutes. For days, weeks, months if needed.

Let it become a companion:
- Morning: Meet the question
- Throughout the day: Return to it
- Evening: Rest in it
- Before sleep: Let it work in the depths

**3. Don't try to answer it**

The moment you think you have the answer, you've made it conceptual again.

**The practice is staying with the not-knowing.**

**4. Notice when you're "figuring"**

The conceptual mind will try to solve it. Notice this. Smile at it. Return to the question itself.

**Not:** "What do I think about this question?"

**But:** "What happens when I hold this question?"

**5. Let it exhaust you**

These questions are designed to tire out the conceptual mind.

When you've tried every angle and found no answer, when you're completely exhausted—that's when something might shift.

**Not because exhaustion is the goal, but because exhaustion of conceptual grasping creates space for direct seeing.**

### Signs of Progress

**Not "getting the answer," but:**

- The question becomes more alive, not less
- You're less sure of what you know
- Moments of confusion deepen
- Thinking slows down
- More space appears
- The question and you become less separate
- Suddenly: the question disappears, not because you answered it, but because the questioner dissolved

### When Nothing Happens

**This is normal.**

Some people work with koans for years before breakthrough. Others have sudden shifts.

**The practice itself is valuable:**
- It trains attention
- It develops comfort with not-knowing
- It weakens conceptual fixation
- It creates conditions for recognition

**But you can't force insight.**

Continue the practice. Stay curious. Let it ripen in its own time.

## The Meta-Question

**Here's the ultimate paradox:**

If there's no separate self, who's asking these questions?

If non-duality is true, who needs to recognize it?

If you already are what you're looking for, why look?

**These questions point to themselves.**

The very asking reveals the assumption of separation. The very seeking creates the sense of something missing.

**And yet:** The asking itself can undo the separation. The seeking can reveal that there's nowhere to go.

**The koan of the spiritual path:**

You're already what you're seeking. But until you realize this, you must seek. When you realize it, you see you never left home.

## Integration: Living With Questions

**As these practices deepen:**

**Life becomes more question-like:**
- Less certainty about what you know
- More comfort with mystery
- Fewer fixed answers
- More alive presence

**Not:** "I've figured it out"

**But:** "Ah, I don't know—and that's okay"

**Problems become koans:**
- Instead of rushing to solve them
- You live with them
- You let them work on you
- Solutions arise naturally, not from forcing

**Relationships become inquiry:**
- Less assuming you know the other person
- More actual meeting
- Fewer fixed ideas
- More genuine presence

## A Daily Practice

**Morning koan:**

Choose your question. Spend 10-20 minutes sitting with it.

Not thinking about it. Just holding it. Being with it.

**Throughout the day:**

When you notice yourself certain about something, ask:
- "What is this, really?"
- "Do I actually know?"
- "What's here before my certainty?"

**Evening reflection:**

What remained unknown today? What couldn't be figured out? What stayed mysterious?

**Appreciate the not-knowing.**

## The Dissolution

**Eventually, maybe:**

You're sitting with your question. You've held it for days, weeks, months.

And then...

The question dissolves.

Not because you answered it.

But because the questioner can no longer be found.

**What remains:**

Just this. Just now. Just being.

No question. No questioner. No answer needed.

**This isn't the end of inquiry.**

It's the beginning of living from the question rather than trying to escape it.
