# The Three Faces of Being

> What Most Non-Duality Teachings Miss

*This article presents what may be the most crucial framework for understanding non-duality in a way that doesn't collapse into reductionism, bypassing, or confusion. It's simple to state, profound to contemplate, and transformative to live from.*

## The Essential Insight

From one sense or perspective, **you are nothing at all**.

From another sense or perspective, **you are everything, everywhere, all at once**.

From yet another sense or perspective, **you are an individual person**, with a body, mind, story, and journey.

**These are all true simultaneously.**

Yet stating them—even all three together—still isn't Truth itself. They're perspectives, each revealing something real while remaining partial.

## Why This Matters

Most spiritual teachings, including many approaches to non-duality, get stuck emphasizing one perspective while dismissing or minimizing the others. This creates predictable problems:

**Over-emphasizing the Absolute** (Nothing):
- "There's no self, so nothing really matters"
- Spiritual bypassing and dissociation
- Loss of embodiment and engagement

**Over-emphasizing the Universal** (Everything):
- "We're all One, so boundaries are illusions"
- Grandiosity and loss of personal responsibility
- Conflating non-separation with sameness

**Over-emphasizing the Relative** (Individual):
- Getting lost in the story of "me"
- Missing the liberation available in seeing beyond separate selfhood
- Treating development as identity rather than as unfolding capacity

**The magic happens when you can hold all three perspectives as simultaneously true**—fluidly moving between them as appropriate, without clinging to any single view as ultimate.

## The First Face: You Are Nothing

### What This Means

From the perspective of pure awareness, there is no separate, solid, continuous entity that you can point to and say "This is me."

When you look for yourself directly:
- Where exactly are you?
- Can you find a boundary between awareness and what it's aware of?
- Is there actually a separate observer, or just observing happening?
- What remains when all content of experience is stripped away?

What you find (or more accurately, what finds itself) is **no-thing**—not an object, not a thing, not a separate entity. Just awareness, aware. Consciousness conscious. Being being.

This is the great "emptiness" or "void" that contemplatives point to. Not empty in the sense of lacking anything, but empty of separate selfhood. No center. No boundary. No birth. No death. Just this—aware, luminous, present.

### The Wisdom This Perspective Offers

**Freedom from existential anxiety:** When there's no separate self to defend or maintain, the fundamental anxiety of existence dissolves. You can't lose what you never were.

**Release from the project of self-completion:** The endless quest to make yourself whole, successful, lovable, or enlightened is seen as unnecessary. You were never incomplete.

**Immediate presence:** Without the overlay of a separate self constantly interpreting and defending, there's just this—whatever is arising, exactly as it is.

### The Tradition That Emphasizes This

**Advaita Vedanta**, particularly in its direct path teachings, often emphasizes this perspective. "Neti neti" (not this, not this)—whatever you can observe is not the true Self. What remains when all that can be observed is negated? Pure consciousness, the witness, awareness itself.

### The Danger of Over-Emphasizing Only This

When this becomes the ONLY perspective:
- Life can become flat, dissociated, meaningless
- Psychological work gets bypassed ("There's no one here to heal")
- Relationships become impersonal ("It's all just appearance")
- Responsibilities are shirked ("Who's there to be responsible?")
- Growth stops ("Development is for the ego")

This is **spiritual bypassing** at its most insidious—using the absolute truth to avoid relative realities.

## The Second Face: You Are Everything

### What This Means

From another perspective, what you are is not separate from the totality of existence. You're not a fragment looking at the whole—you ARE the whole, temporarily experiencing itself as a fragment.

The boundary between "self" and "world" is seen as functional rather than fundamental. You're not in the universe—you're continuous with it. The air you breathe, the food you eat, the sensory information flowing in, the thoughts arising—all are movements of the same undivided reality.

Every other being, every object, every phenomenon—none are ultimately separate from what you fundamentally are. The other is not ultimately other. The universe isn't something you're in; it's what you are, looking out through these particular eyes, thinking through this particular mind.

### The Wisdom This Perspective Offers

**Radical interconnection:** The ecological insight that everything is interwoven becomes felt reality rather than just intellectual understanding. Harming "another" is harming yourself. Healing "another" is healing yourself.

**Cosmic perspective:** Your individual concerns are held within a vast context. You're both infinitesimally small (one perspective among billions) and infinitely large (the totality experiencing itself).

**Natural compassion:** When the other isn't ultimately other, compassion isn't forced—it's the natural response of awareness recognizing itself everywhere.

### The Tradition That Emphasizes This

**Mystical traditions across religions** often emphasize this—Christian mystics speaking of union with God, Sufi poets describing the Beloved everywhere, Hindu teachings of "Tat Tvam Asi" (You are That), the Buddhist recognition of interdependent origination.

### The Danger of Over-Emphasizing Only This

When this becomes the ONLY perspective:
- Personal boundaries dissolve inappropriately
- Individual responsibility becomes unclear
- The unique expression of your particular life is devalued
- "Spiritual oneness" can justify overriding others' autonomy
- Grandiosity can develop ("I am the Universe!")

This can lead to what spiritual teacher John Welwood called **"pseudo-oneness"**—claiming universal consciousness while avoiding the particular work of being this human.

## The Third Face: You Are Someone

### What This Means

You are also genuinely, really, importantly **this particular human being**. With this specific body, this unique history, these particular gifts and limitations, this developmental journey.

You have a name, a story, relationships, responsibilities, wounds that need healing, capacities that need developing, growth that needs happening. This isn't less real or less important than the other perspectives—it's a different level of truth.

Your personality isn't an illusion to transcend—it's the unique way awareness expresses through this particular form. Your developmental stage isn't something to escape—it's your current capacity for making meaning. Your psychological patterns aren't obstacles to enlightenment—they're the actual territory where recognition gets integrated.

### The Wisdom This Perspective Offers

**Embodied humanity:** You're not trying to be a disembodied witness or a cosmic everything—you're learning to be THIS human, fully and authentically.

**Appropriate boundaries:** You can care for this body, protect this time, honor these needs—not from ego-defense but from practical wisdom.

**Developmental respect:** You recognize that you're at a particular stage of development, with specific capacities and limitations. This isn't a problem—it's where the work happens.

**Psychological integration:** Your trauma needs healing, your patterns need attention, your relationships need care. This isn't bypassed by spiritual recognition—it's where spiritual recognition gets lived.

### The Tradition That Emphasizes This

**Developmental psychology, humanistic psychology, and therapeutic traditions** naturally emphasize this perspective. So do earth-centered spiritualities that honor embodiment, and contemporary integral approaches that take development seriously.

### The Danger of Over-Emphasizing Only This

When this becomes the ONLY perspective:
- You can become completely identified with your story
- Existential anxiety has no relief
- The liberation of seeing beyond separate selfhood is missed
- Life becomes heavy with the weight of "me and my problems"
- Spiritual seeking becomes just another self-improvement project

This is the default state for most people—stuck in the story of being a separate self, never recognizing the other dimensions.

## The Integration: All Three Are True Simultaneously

Here's where it gets really interesting: **You don't have to choose.**

In any given moment, you are:
- **Nothing** (pure awareness, no separate self)
- **Everything** (not separate from the totality)
- **Someone** (this unique human expression)

These aren't contradictory—they're complementary perspectives on the same reality, like different angles on a multifaceted jewel.

### How They Work Together

**The Absolute (Nothing) provides the foundation:**
You are awareness itself—this is the context for everything else. From here, there's fundamental freedom, no existential threat, no need to complete yourself.

**The Universal (Everything) provides the connection:**
You're not an isolated awareness—you're awareness that's continuous with all existence. From here comes compassion, ecological understanding, and the recognition that serving others is serving yourself.

**The Relative (Someone) provides the expression:**
Awareness expresses through this particular human form, with its unique qualities, developmental level, psychological patterns, and life journey. From here comes appropriate action, embodied living, and the actual work of being human.

### Moving Fluidly Between Perspectives

Wisdom isn't holding one perspective—it's knowing which perspective is appropriate when:

**When to rest in Nothing:**
- When you're overwhelmed by the story of "me"
- When you need freedom from identification
- When you're trapped in anxiety about the future or regret about the past
- When you need to remember what you fundamentally are

**When to recognize Everything:**
- When you're feeling isolated or separate
- When compassion needs to arise
- When you need ecological perspective
- When you're judging or othering someone

**When to honor Someone:**
- When you need to take care of this body
- When psychological work needs doing
- When boundaries need setting
- When responsibilities need meeting
- When relationships need tending
- When development is calling

### A Practical Example

Let's say you're dealing with difficult emotions after a conflict:

**From Nothing:** You recognize these emotions as movements in awareness. There's space for them. They don't define you. They arise and pass like weather. This provides peace and perspective.

**From Everything:** You recognize that the other person's pain and your pain are not ultimately separate. What appears as conflict is consciousness playing all the parts. This provides compassion and reduced defensiveness.

**From Someone:** You also recognize that you need to process these emotions, perhaps talk to a friend, maybe set a boundary. This body needs care. This psychological pattern needs attention. This is where the actual work of resolution happens.

**All three perspectives are useful.** Not choosing one over the others.

## How This Relates to Spiral Dynamics

Different developmental stages naturally emphasize different faces:

**Purple** (Tribal): Tends toward the Universal through tribal belonging—"We are the clan"

**Red** (Power): Strongly emphasizes Someone—"I am this powerful individual"

**Blue** (Traditional): Balances Someone and Universal through devotion—"I am a servant of something greater"

**Orange** (Achievement): Strongly emphasizes Someone—"I am this successful individual making my mark"

**Green** (Community): Leans toward Universal—"We are all connected" while honoring Someone through unique expression

**Yellow** (Systemic): Can hold all three perspectives and see how they relate—this article is being written from Yellow

**Turquoise** (Holistic): Naturally rests in the integration of all three, moving fluidly as appropriate

**The point:** Your developmental stage influences which perspective feels most natural or accessible. But the recognition of all three is available at any stage—it just gets expressed through your current developmental capacity.

## Common Questions

### "Which perspective is most true?"

This is the wrong question. They're not competing for truth—they're different levels of truth. It's like asking whether you're truly a human, or truly made of cells, or truly made of atoms. Yes to all, at different scales of analysis.

### "Should I practice seeing from all three perspectives?"

Not as a practice, but as a recognition. These aren't perspectives you adopt—they're perspectives that are already true. The work is noticing what's already the case, not manufacturing something new.

### "What if I can only access one perspective?"

That's completely normal. Most people have one that feels most natural (often based on their developmental stage and temperament). The invitation isn't to force all three but to remain open to the others as they reveal themselves.

### "Isn't this just making things more complicated?"

Initially, perhaps. But actually, this framework SIMPLIFIES things by showing that many spiritual debates are just people emphasizing different perspectives and thinking they're mutually exclusive.

Person A: "There is no self!" (Nothing perspective)
Person B: "But I clearly exist as this individual!" (Someone perspective)

Both are right. They're pointing to different faces of the same jewel.

## The Living Recognition

Here's the ultimate paradox: the recognition that all three are true simultaneously is itself none of the three perspectives—it's the **living recognition that gives rise to all perspectives.**

You can't grasp this intellectually. You can only notice what's already true. Right now:

- Is there awareness? (Nothing)
- Is there experience? (Everything)
- Is there a human reading these words? (Someone)

Yes, yes, and yes. All at once. Not as separate facts but as inseparable faces of being.

## Moving Forward

This framework of three faces becomes invaluable as you explore further:

- It prevents spiritual bypassing (you can't use Nothing to avoid Someone)
- It prevents pseudo-oneness (you can't use Everything to bypass Someone)
- It prevents getting lost in story (Someone is held within Nothing and Everything)
- It provides a map for where different teachings are pointing
- It shows how development and awakening relate
