# Noticing the Noticer

> Turning attention back on itself to recognize awareness

*You notice things all day long—sights, sounds, thoughts, sensations. But what notices the noticing? This article explores a gradual, gentle practice of turning attention back toward its source. Not forcing, not grasping, but simply becoming curious about what's aware right now. This is recognition through investigation rather than through sudden breakthrough.*

## The Basic Principle

**Usually, attention flows outward:**
- To objects in the visual field
- To sounds arising
- To thoughts in mind
- To sensations in body
- To tasks at hand

**This is natural and necessary.** But attention can also turn back on itself.

Not by creating a new object called "awareness" to observe, but by recognizing what's already observing everything else.

**The practice:** Gently, repeatedly, notice what's noticing.

## The First Turn: Noticing That You're Noticing

**Right now:**

You're reading these words. You're aware of them.

**But:** Are you aware that you're aware?

**This is the first turn—meta-awareness.**

Not just aware, but aware OF awareness.

**Try this:**
1. Notice something simple (the sensation of breathing)
2. Notice that you're noticing it
3. Rest in that second-order awareness

**What do you find?**

Most people discover a subtle shift:
- From being absorbed in the object
- To recognizing the awareness of the object
- A slight "stepping back" into witnessing

**This isn't creating distance.** It's recognizing what was already here—the awareness that makes noticing possible.

## The Second Turn: What's Aware of Awareness?

**Now take it deeper:**

You're aware that you're aware. Good.

**But:** What's aware of that awareness?

**Try this:**
1. Notice you're aware (first turn)
2. Notice what's aware of that awareness (second turn)
3. And what's aware of THAT? (third turn)

**This creates a recursion:**
- Awareness aware of awareness
- Aware of being aware of awareness
- Aware of being aware of being aware...

**The mind might protest:** "This is infinite! How do I stop?"

**The answer:** You don't stop. You recognize that there's no separate observer at each level. Just awareness, aware of itself, appearing as if there are levels.

**What you might discover:**

All the "levels" collapse into one seamless awareness. The observer, the observed, and the observing are not actually separate.

## The Practice: Returning Attention to Its Source

**This is the core practice.**

### Step 1: Notice Any Object

Pick anything:
- A sound
- A sensation  
- A thought
- A visual object

Notice it clearly.

### Step 2: Shift From Object to Subject

Don't just notice the object. Notice what's noticing it.

**Not as a concept:** "Awareness is noticing this"

**But as recognition:** "What is this noticing?"

### Step 3: Rest As the Subject

Stay with the awareness itself rather than what it's aware of.

**This is subtle.** You're not creating a new object called "awareness." You're recognizing what's already here as the subject of all experience.

### Step 4: Notice When Attention Wanders

It will. Attention will get absorbed in objects again—thoughts, sensations, sounds.

**When you notice this:** That very noticing is awareness. You're already there.

Simply return: What's aware right now?

### Step 5: Repeat

This practice is simple but not easy. The habit of attention flowing outward is strong.

**The practice:** Again and again, gently turn attention toward its source.

Not forcing. Not straining. Just... noticing the noticer.

## Common Experiences

### "I can't find what's noticing"

**Good!** You're looking correctly.

Awareness isn't an object you can find. It's what's doing the looking.

**It's like the eye trying to see itself.** Not because the eye doesn't exist, but because it's the subject, not an object.

**When you can't find it:** That not-finding is actually the recognition. You can't find it because you ARE it.

### "I feel like I'm in my head"

This is common. There's often a sense of awareness being located behind the eyes, in the head.

**Try this:**
1. Notice that sense of being "in the head"
2. What's aware of that sensation of being located?
3. Is that awareness itself located anywhere?

**Often what happens:** The sense of location loosens. Awareness is revealed as not being in any particular place—it's more like the space in which locations appear.

### "I go back and forth between object and subject"

**Perfect!** This is the practice.

- Attention gets absorbed in objects (normal)
- You notice and return to awareness (the practice)
- Attention gets absorbed again (normal)
- You return again (the practice)

**This is not a problem.** This IS the practice. The returning itself is what strengthens recognition.

### "Sometimes there's a shift—it's clear, then it's gone"

Yes! This is common. Brief moments of clear recognition followed by the habitual return to identifying with thoughts, sensations, the person.

**Don't chase the clear moments.** Don't try to maintain them.

Just keep practicing the return. The recognition will stabilize naturally over time.

### "I feel like there's spaciousness"

Some people experience awareness as vast, open, spacious—not confined to the body or head.

**This is fine.** Just don't make it into a special state to seek. Awareness is here whether it feels spacious or ordinary.

**The recognition doesn't depend on particular experiences.**

## Variations of the Practice

### With Thought

**When a thought arises:**
1. Notice the thought
2. Notice you're aware of it
3. Rest as what's aware

**Don't try to stop thoughts.** Just recognize what's aware of them.

**What you might discover:** Thoughts lose their compelling quality. They're just movements in awareness, like clouds in the sky.

### With Emotion

**When emotion arises:**
1. Notice the emotion (without naming if possible)
2. Notice the sensations that make up the emotion
3. Notice what's aware of these sensations
4. Rest as that awareness

**The shift:** From "I am angry" to "anger is arising in awareness." This isn't dissociation—it's accurate. Awareness isn't angry; emotion is arising IN awareness.

### With Perception

**Looking at an object:**
1. Notice the visual appearance
2. Notice you're seeing it
3. Notice what's doing the seeing
4. Can you find a boundary between seer and seen?

**What often emerges:** The seer and the seen aren't two separate things. There's just seeing happening—awareness appearing as both subject and object.

### With Sensation

**Feeling a sensation:**
1. Notice the sensation clearly
2. Notice you're aware of it
3. Notice: Where is awareness located in relation to the sensation?
4. Is awareness separate from sensation or is sensation arising IN awareness?

**The discovery:** Sensations don't happen "to you"—they happen IN the awareness that you are.

## The Deepening Practice

### Resting As Awareness

After you've practiced turning attention to its source many times, you might find you can rest there.

**This isn't a state to achieve.** It's more like... stopping the outward movement and recognizing what remains.

**Instructions:**
1. Turn attention to what's aware
2. Rest there
3. Don't do anything with it
4. Just be awareness, aware of being awareness

**How long?** As long as is comfortable. Even a few seconds is valuable.

### Allowing Everything in Awareness

**A paradox:**

When you rest as awareness, objects don't disappear. Thoughts, sensations, perceptions continue.

**But now:** They're recognized as arising IN awareness rather than TO a separate you.

**The practice:**
- Rest as awareness
- Allow everything to be as it is
- Notice that awareness is not disturbed by what arises in it
- Like the sky undisturbed by weather, or the screen undisturbed by the movie

### The Seamless Recognition

**Eventually, perhaps:**

There's no longer a sense of "turning attention" or "resting as awareness."

**Instead:** Just awareness, knowing itself, appearing as this moment.

No separate one doing the noticing. Just noticing happening. Awareness aware.

**This isn't a special state.** It's the ordinary nature of this moment, recognized clearly.

## Integration With Daily Life

**The practice isn't just for meditation.**

### Micro-Practices Throughout the Day

**Every hour (set a gentle reminder):**
- Pause whatever you're doing
- Notice: What's aware right now?
- Brief recognition (even 3 seconds)
- Continue with what you were doing

**The effect:** Little moments of recognition throughout the day. The pattern of awareness of awareness becomes more familiar.

### With Routine Activities

**Choose one routine activity:** Washing dishes, making coffee, brushing teeth, walking to car.

**During this activity:**
- Notice the sensations, movements, perceptions
- Notice what's aware of them
- Practice this every time you do this activity

**This anchors the practice to daily life** rather than keeping it only in meditation.

### In Conversation

**While listening to someone:**
- Hear their words
- Notice you're hearing them
- Notice what's aware
- Remain present to them while also resting as awareness

**This doesn't create distance.** Often you become MORE present to the other person when you're not completely absorbed in your reactions to them.

### With Difficult Moments

**When something challenging arises:**
- Feel the reaction (don't bypass)
- Notice what's aware of the reaction
- Rest there briefly
- Respond from that awareness rather than from the reaction alone

**This creates space** between stimulus and response. Not by suppressing the reaction but by recognizing the awareness that contains it.

## The Difference From Dissociation

**Important clarification:**

This practice can sound like dissociation—creating distance from experience. It's not.

**Dissociation:**
- Creates distance from feelings to avoid them
- Numbs or disconnects
- Makes you less present, less engaged
- Is a defense mechanism

**Awareness recognition:**
- Includes all feelings without resistance
- Is radically present and intimate
- Makes you MORE sensitive and engaged
- Is not defensive—nothing to defend

**The test:**
- Does this practice make you more able to feel and be present?
- Or less able?

If less—you might be using "awareness" language to dissociate. Return to feeling everything while recognizing what's aware of it.

## Combining With Other Practices

**This practice works well with:**

### Breath Awareness

Notice breathing. Then notice what's aware of breathing. Rest there.

### Body Scanning

Scan the body. Each time you notice sensation, notice what's aware of it.

### Meditation

Whatever your meditation practice, add this dimension: notice what's aware of the practice itself.

### Self-Inquiry

"Who am I?" naturally leads to noticing the noticer. Who's asking? What's aware of the asking?

**These practices support each other.** Use whichever gateway is most accessible in any moment.

## Subtle Signs of Deepening

**Not dramatic shifts, but subtle changes:**

**Thoughts lose stickiness:**
- They still arise
- But they're more transparent
- Less compelling
- Easier to let pass

**Emotions have more space:**
- They still arise (sometimes strongly)
- But there's space around them
- They're held in awareness
- They don't consume you completely

**Sense of self loosens:**
- The "me" story becomes lighter
- Not that it disappears
- But it's recognized as story rather than solid fact
- More fluidity, less fixation

**Present moment becomes obvious:**
- Not as a concept
- But as what's always already here
- Less sense of being in time
- More sense of time appearing in you

**Fundamental okayness:**
- Not that everything is pleasant
- But a basic sense that awareness itself is unthreatened
- Problems remain, but the existential anxiety decreases
- More fundamental trust

## Common Questions

### "How long should I practice before something happens?"

**There's no timeline.** Some people have clear recognition quickly. Others practice for months or years.

**But:** Every moment of noticing the noticer is already the recognition. Don't wait for some future moment when it "finally happens."

**The practice itself is the recognition, gradually stabilizing.**

### "Is this the same as 'I Am' awareness?"

Similar! "I Am" is the sense of being before any content—before "I am this" or "I am that."

Noticing the noticer often reveals this "I Am" presence—the simple sense of being aware, without attributes.

**But don't grasp the experience.** Just recognize what's here.

### "What if I can't do this while meditating?"

**That's okay!** Some people find it easier during daily activities than in formal meditation.

**Try it:**
- While walking
- While in nature
- While doing simple tasks
- While listening to music

**Find what works for you.** There's no rule that this must happen in sitting meditation.

### "Is this about becoming the witness?"

**Not exactly.** The witness is often experienced as a position—"I am the witness of experience."

**This practice can start there,** but it deepens beyond that:
- First: Identifying as the witness (subject)
- Then: Recognizing that even the witness is witnessed
- Finally: No separate witness—just awareness, aware

**The witness is a useful stage, not the final recognition.**

### "Can I practice this too much?"

**Generally no.** Brief, frequent returns to awareness are more effective than long, forced sessions.

**But:** If you notice you're using this practice to avoid life, dial it back. The recognition should make you more engaged, not less.

## A Structured Practice Session

**For those who like structure:**

### 5-10 Minute Session

**Minutes 1-2: Notice objects**
- Sounds, sensations, thoughts
- Just noticing what's arising
- No need to focus—just aware of the field

**Minutes 3-4: Notice the noticer**
- For each object noticed
- Turn attention to what's noticing
- Rest briefly as awareness

**Minutes 5-6: Rest as awareness**
- Stop turning
- Just rest as what's aware
- Allow everything to be as it is

**Minutes 7-10: Open awareness**
- Rest as awareness
- While remaining open to all experience
- Neither seeking nor avoiding anything
- Just being awareness, knowing itself

**End:** Notice how you feel. Return to daily life.

### Daily Rhythm

**Morning:** 5-10 minute practice as described above

**Midday:** Brief check-ins (1 minute each, several times)

**Evening:** 5-10 minute practice

**Before sleep:** Notice what's aware as you fall asleep

**This rhythm** builds familiarity without making it feel like a burden.

## The Recognition

**At some point, perhaps:**

You notice the noticer and realize:

**You've always been what you're noticing.**

Not that you "become" awareness. You recognize you never were anything else.

**The thoughts, sensations, perceptions—**
They're not happening TO you.
They're happening IN you.
They ARE you—awareness appearing as temporary forms.

**The separate self—**
Also arising in awareness.
Not who you are, but what's appearing.

**What remains:**
Just this.
Awareness, aware.
Knowing itself as this moment.

**Nothing special. Nothing missing.**

Just... noticing.
