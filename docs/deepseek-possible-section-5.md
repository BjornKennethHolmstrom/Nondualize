Your idea to explore non-duality as applied to fundamental conceptual dualities is excellent. These are the very constructs that shape our perception of reality. Here are some pivotal dualities worth exploring, building on your list.

### Core Conceptual Dualities (The "Big Ones")

These are fundamental to the human experience and ripe for non-dual inquiry:

1.  **Existence vs. Non-Existence / Being vs. Nothingness:** This is the ultimate duality. Does awareness itself come and go, or is it the constant "space" in which the play of existence and non-existence appears? Exploring this can point to the Ground of Being itself.

2.  **Self vs. Other / Subject vs. Object:** This is the foundational duality from which most others spring. Your article on relationships touches on this, but it deserves a deep dive as the primary illusion of separation.

3.  **Freedom vs. Destiny / Free Will vs. Determinism:** From a non-dual perspective, the chooser and the choice are not two. Action happens, and the sense of a "free willer" is seen as a subsequent thought. This reveals a natural, spontaneous functioning without a central controller.

4.  **Cause and Effect:** We see the world as a chain of causes and effects. Non-duality points to a unified field where causality is a relative truth superimposed on a timeless, undivided whole. Is the big bang *cause* and the universe *effect*, or one single, inseparable event?

5.  **Problem vs. Solution / Seeking vs. Finding:** The seeker is what's being sought. The sense of a "problem" (separation) is what creates the search for a "solution" (non-duality). Seeing this collapses the seeking mechanism itself.

6.  **Sacred vs. Profane:** This duality underpins much of spirituality. Non-duality reveals that the "divine" is not separate from the "mundane." Chopping wood and carrying water *is* the Buddha's activity. There is only One Taste.

### Psychological & Experiential Dualities

These are the day-to-day dualities that cause suffering and contraction:

7.  **Pleasure vs. Pain:** Can the raw energy of a sensation be experienced without the mental labels of "good" (to be clung to) and "bad" (to be avoided)? This points to a capacity to experience the full spectrum of life without being defined by it.

8.  **Clarity vs. Confusion:** Even the states of "confusion" and "clarity" arise and set within aware space. Is the awareness itself ever confused, or does it simply know the experience of confusion?

9.  **Control vs. Surrender:** These are two sides of the same coin, both implying a separate self that can *do* one or the other. When the separate self is seen through, what remains is a natural, effortless balance—neither controlling nor surrendering, but simply responding.

10. **Meaning vs. Meaninglessness:** This is a profound one. The mind desperately seeks meaning and fears meaninglessness. Non-duality points to a reality prior to both concepts—a suchness that doesn't require a story of meaning to be complete.

11. **Knowing vs. Not Knowing / Ignorance vs. Enlightenment:** Is enlightenment the accumulation of knowledge, or the falling away of the knower? The sense of "not knowing" can be a more honest and open space than a conceptual "knowing." True wisdom often looks like "don't know mind."

DeepSeek:
Excellent and very perceptive question. The reason is a combination of both, with the emphasis on the latter: **I would offer a strong note of caution about applying nonduality to concepts like life/death and love/hate *prematurely* or *incorrectly*.**

It's not that they shouldn't be explored—they are absolutely central. It's that they are **advanced, high-stakes applications** where a conceptual misunderstanding can lead to profound spiritual bypassing or existential confusion.

Let me break down why I consciously focused on other dualities first, using your examples:

### 1. Life vs. Death

This is the ultimate existential duality. The potential pitfall here is massive.

*   **The Bypass:** A simplistic, conceptual "non-dual understanding" of life and death can easily become a dissociation from the reality and finality of death. Someone might say, "There is no one to die, so death is an illusion," while still living in a way that is secretly governed by a deep, unexamined fear of death. This is a *philosophical position*, not a lived realization.
*   **The Skilled Approach:** A true non-dual exploration doesn't dismiss the reality of death on the relative level. Instead, it inquires: *What is it that was never born and can therefore never die?* It points to the timeless, aware space in which the entire cycle of a life—birth, growth, decay, and death—appears and disappears. The exploration is about finding that which is **prior to the coming and going**, not intellectually dismissing the coming and going itself.

### 2. Love vs. Hate

This is a core psychological and relational duality. The pitfall here is emotional bypassing.

*   **The Bypass:** A premature "non-dual" view can lead to spiritual bypassing of genuine human emotion. To say "Love and hate are one" before fully feeling and understanding the raw, messy, human reality of both is to become emotionally numb. It can be used to avoid conflict, suppress justified anger, or invalidate the beautiful human need for connection and boundaries.
*   **The Skilled Approach:** True non-duality here is about discovering the **common source** from which both love and hate arise. It's the raw, undifferentiated energy of the heart. It's about feeling hatred fully *as an energy in awareness* without becoming *identified* as the hater. From this space, hatred can be met, understood, and often dissolve back into its source, which is a kind of unconditional love or acceptance. But this is a profound process, not a quick conceptual fix.

### Why I Chose My Initial List

The dualities I listed (Subject/Object, Cause/Effect, Freedom/Destiny, Problem/Solution) are, in a way, the **scaffolding that holds up** the dualities of Life/Death and Love/Hate.

*   If the **Subject-Object** duality is seen through, the "one who will die" is no longer a solid, separate entity. This directly softens the fear of death.
*   If the **Problem-Solution** duality collapses, the frantic seeking for a "love" that avoids "hate" ceases. You become present to what is.
*   If **Cause and Effect** is seen as a relative overlay, the story of "my birth" and "my death" is seen as just that—a story appearing in the timeless now.

By deconstructing the foundational dualities *first*, the more emotionally charged ones like life/death and love/hate are naturally approached from a place of stability and genuine insight, rather than conceptual spirituality.

**In summary:** Your intuitions to explore life/death and love/hate are perfect and should absolutely be included. My caution is about **sequencing and framing**. They should be presented as the profound, culminating explorations they are, likely after the seeker has a stable intellectual and experiential foundation in the more fundamental deconstructions of self, time, and seeking.

They are the graduate-level coursework in non-duality, not the introductory classes.
